Open these sections, not front pages

Front pages are event reporting. These sections are argument pieces — one mechanism each. Tap, scan three headlines, apply the filter, pick one.
WSJ5 min
Heard on the Street
600 words, one analytical claim per piece. The single best use of a five-minute gap you have.
ECONOMIST15 min
Finance & Economics
The densest mechanism-per-minute section in journalism. Two or three pieces a week are worth your time; the section tells you which.

Weekly. Takes one market puzzle and explains why it exists. Closest thing in print to a valuation interview answer.

One long piece a day on an Indian business, always mechanism-first. Highest value per article in your whole list — but it needs a real gap, not a corridor.

This week, look for

I can't see inside the Economist, WSJ, Mint or ET — they block automated access entirely. So instead of naming articles, here is what to scan for when you open those sections this week. If a headline touches one of these, it is worth your gap.

1. Whether the RBI's $80 billion FCNR(B) inflow target is actually reached before the window closes on 31 August. $56.85 billion is in as of the governor's Thursday interview. Watch for the final tally once the scheme shuts, and whether the rupee moves at all once the artificial support ends.

2. Whether NSE's IPO price band gets formally locked in line with the ₹5.26 trillion roadshow number. SEBI approval on the draft prospectus is expected by end-August, with launch targeted for the second half of September. Watch for the first officially filed price band, and whether it still implies the same ~₹2,100/share this week's Deal Desk worked out.

3. Whether India's 1 mt sugar import actually pulls ex-mill prices down by the ₹500/quintal industry insiders expect. Current price is ₹5,400-5,560/quintal in Maharashtra. Watch for the first post-import price print — a bigger or smaller move than ₹500 tells you something about how tight the real supply gap is.

4. Whether FMCG companies' Q2 commentary confirms the margin pressure they flagged this week. Packaging and palm oil costs were the risk cited across Marico, Dabur, GCPL and Jyothy Labs. Watch for any early guidance cut or margin warning as Q2 approaches.

Capture — one line, right now

Not a summary. A claim you could defend. If you can't write one, the article wasn't worth reading, and you just learned something about your filter.
