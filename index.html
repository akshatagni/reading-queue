Open these sections, not front pages

Front pages are event reporting. These sections are argument pieces — one mechanism each. Tap, scan three headlines, apply the filter, pick one.
WSJ5 min
Heard on the Street
600 words, one analytical claim per piece. The single best use of a five-minute gap you have.
ECONOMIST15 min
Finance & Economics
The densest mechanism-per-minute section in journalism. Two or three pieces a week are worth your time; the section tells you which.
ECONOMIST15 min
Buttonwood — markets column
Weekly. Takes one market puzzle and explains why it exists. Closest thing in print to a valuation interview answer.
ECONOMIST15 min
Schumpeter — business strategy column
Corporate strategy, one company or one idea per week. Maps directly onto your STRG coursework and onto consulting cases.
ECONOMIST15 min
Free Exchange — economics column
Usually explains a piece of research. Good for NMEU and MGEC reinforcement.
ECONOMIST5 min
The World This Week
One page, the whole week. Use this when you have five minutes and no appetite for depth.
THE KEN25-30 min
The daily story
One long piece a day on an Indian business, always mechanism-first. Highest value per article in your whole list — but it needs a real gap, not a corridor.
THE KEN12 min
Long and Short
Shorter Ken format. Markets and company analysis with the numbers shown.
THE KEN12 min
Ka-Ching — fintech and lending
Banking, NBFCs, payments. Three of your casebook industry primers live here, and it is where Indian finance interviews get their questions.
ET PRIME20 min
ET Prime — long-form only
Skip the ET front page entirely. It is event reporting at low value per minute. Prime is where the analysis lives.
MINT10 min
Plain Facts
Data-led explainers on the Indian economy. Charts you can quote in a GD.
MINT20 min
Mint Long Story
Weekly deep dive, usually an industry. Good complement to your casebook primers.
BS7 min
Business Standard — Opinion
Not paywalled. Indian policy and markets commentary, and the source your daily brief is built from.

This week, look for

I can't see inside the Economist, WSJ, Mint or ET — they block automated access entirely. So instead of naming articles, here is what to scan for when you open those sections this week. If a headline touches one of these, it is worth your gap.

1. Whether Brent actually keeps climbing toward $115. One analyst's call if the US-Iran standoff persists into October-November. Watch whether OMC pump prices move, or the government absorbs the under-recovery instead, that choice is the current-account-versus-fiscal-deficit trade-off from today's brief, playing out in real time.

2. NSE's updated DRHP and final price band, expected early next week. Today's brief has it provisionally at ₹1,700-1,800 with a ₹228 grey market premium. Watch whether the OFS size actually gets trimmed to 5.25%, and whether the GMP holds up once the price band is locked.

3. Whether the rupee tests 95, or the RBI's dollar sales hold the line first. It closed at 94.83 today after its worst single-day fall in eight weeks. The US Fed's FOMC meets 15-16 September, watch for any commentary linking the two.

4. Whether the IDBI Bank stake sale to Fairfax actually moves forward. It hinges on Fairfax first offloading its IIFL Finance stake to clear a regulatory conflict. If that clears, a single deal would cover more than a third of the government's entire FY27 disinvestment target.

Capture — one line, right now

Not a summary. A claim you could defend. If you can't write one, the article wasn't worth reading, and you just learned something about your filter.

Every Sunday, paste your saved lines into chat. I turn the week into three positions you hold, each with evidence and its strongest counter-argument. That is the part that makes you stand out — not knowing facts, but having views you can defend.
